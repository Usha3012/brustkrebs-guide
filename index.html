The first look

On 9 April my Hausarzt examined me. I had no symptoms, only a small lump that had been there for a few months. But it had become aggressively large over a short time.

I still remember her look, and the rush. She asked reception to put an urgent sticker on my referral. That was the first moment I felt this might not go well.

Everything moved so fast

In Germany we say it takes ages to get an appointment. I had my diagnosis within one month: aggressive breast cancer, stage 3. That speed was itself a clear sign of how serious it was.

My whole world was turned upside down. All that month I had one doctor’s appointment or another, every single day. My thoughts were mixed and overwhelming: the news, the daily visits, the fear of what would happen next.

What helped me

When I asked one of my doctors what to expect, the answer was:

“Imagine the worst of the worst.”

It sounds terrible, but believe me, it worked. It helped me prepare, mentally and physically, and to know who I was going to fight. That is what worked for me. Everyone is different.

Where I am today

It is a long journey, but I am happy to have cleared the first hurdle. On 20 October I am having my 16th and last chemotherapy. The first phase of my treatment is behind me.

Why this guide exists

During this time it was very hard to get all the information in one place. So I am sharing my experience here, and everything you need from start to end, and I will keep adding more and more.

This is my way of celebrating Pinktober and spreading awareness. Awareness not only for the person who needs it, but for the people around them.
