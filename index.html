019 April

The first look

On 9 April my Hausarzt, my family doctor, examined me. I had no symptoms, only a small lump that had been there for a few months. But over a short period of time it had become aggressively large.

I still remember her look, and the rush. The way she asked reception to put an urgent sticker on my referral. That was the first moment I felt this might not go well.

02Within one month

Everything moved so fast

In Germany we say it takes ages to get a doctor’s appointment, and I agree, it often does. But when there is real urgency, they take it very seriously and make things happen.

The cancer was growing at a fast pace. But on the other side, the doctors worked at a pace I can truly recommend. Within one month I had my diagnosis (aggressive breast cancer, stage 3) and my treatment schedule in my hands. That is the positive side of our healthcare, and I want to share it.

03That whole month

A world turned upside down

My whole world was turned upside down. All that month I had one doctor’s appointment or another, every single day. My thoughts were mixed and overwhelming: the news, the daily visits, the fear of what would happen next.

20 October

The first hurdle

It is a long journey, but I am happy to have cleared the first hurdle. On 20 October I am having my 16th and last chemotherapy, which closes the first phase of my treatment. The journey continues, and I am taking it one step at a time.

Why I am sharing this

During this time it was very hard to get all the information in one place. So I am sharing my experience and everything you need from start to end, and I will keep adding more and more. This is my way of celebrating Pinktober and spreading awareness, not only for the person who needs it, but for the people around them.
